Autonomous artificial intelligence for diabetic retinopathy (DR) screening is one of the most mature applications of AI in medicine. This summary frames what is reasonably established and what still requires care. **All sensitivity, specificity and AUC figures below are intentionally omitted pending confirmation against the cleared indication and source literature. [TO CONFIRM]**

## The clinical problem

DR is a leading cause of preventable vision loss, and early detection changes outcomes. Yet many people with diabetes do not receive a timely dilated eye exam. Autonomous screening aims to close that gap by performing a first-line check wherever a patient already is — primary care, pharmacy or retail clinic.

## What "autonomous" means

An autonomous system produces a screening result **without a specialist reading the image in real time**. Cleared systems are bound by a specific *intended use* and a defined imaging protocol; results outside that protocol are not supported.

## Reading the evidence responsibly

When evaluating any screening claim, three questions matter:

1. **Indication match** — does the cited performance correspond to the exact cleared indication and population?
2. **Imaging conditions** — mydriatic vs non-mydriatic, camera type, image-quality gating.
3. **Failure modes** — ungradable rates, false negatives and the safety net for them.

A screening tool is not a diagnostic tool. It does not detect concomitant ophthalmic or systemic disease, and a negative screen does not remove the need for appropriate follow-up care.

## Implications for multispectral imaging

DR involves microvascular and hemorrhagic changes with strong wavelength dependence. In principle, a multispectral record offers AI additional contrast to learn from. **That is a research direction, not a present claim** for any AI-Spectral Technology Corp. product. Any future DR-related capability would be validated, labeled and cleared on its own evidence before being offered.
