The retina is the only place in the body where blood vessels and neural tissue can be observed directly and non-invasively. "Oculomics" is the emerging discipline of mining that view for signals about **systemic health**.

## The premise

Retinal microvasculature reflects the health of the broader vascular system, and the retinal nerve fiber layer is part of the central nervous system. Researchers have explored associations between retinal imaging features and:

- **Cardiovascular risk** — vascular caliber, tortuosity and AI-derived risk scores.
- **Kidney function** — markers correlated with renal measures.
- **Neurological conditions** — including investigational work on neurodegenerative biomarkers.

## An honest assessment of the science

Oculomics is promising and early. Many published associations are real but modest, derived from specific cohorts, and **not yet validated for routine clinical decision-making**. Claims that a retinal photo can "diagnose" a systemic disease are, in 2026, generally premature.

> The responsible position is enthusiasm tempered by validation: treat oculomics as a research frontier, not a marketing headline.

## Why spectral imaging is relevant

Many of the candidate biomarkers are **chromophore-related** — tied to hemoglobin oxygenation, melanin and pigment. These are exactly the signals that vary with wavelength. A multispectral record therefore offers a potentially richer substrate for oculomics research than a single color image.

AI-Spectral Technology Corp.'s interest in oculomics is as a **research enabler**: providing high-quality, multi-wavelength data and tools to the scientists doing this work. We make no systemic-disease diagnostic claims for any current product.
