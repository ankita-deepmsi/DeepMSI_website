The 2025 American Academy of Ophthalmology meeting reinforced a clear direction of travel in retinal imaging: **more of the retina, in more channels, read with more help from software.** A few themes stood out. *[TO CONFIRM: attribute any specific product or vendor claims before publishing.]*

## Widefield and ultra-widefield everywhere

Capturing more of the peripheral retina in a single image continued its march from premium feature to baseline expectation. The clinical argument is simple: pathology does not respect the posterior pole.

## Color fidelity and RGB

There was notable attention to **how faithfully devices reproduce color**, and to multi-channel (RGB and beyond) approaches that separate wavelength information rather than blending it. This is the same intuition that underlies multispectral imaging — that the wavelength a structure responds to is itself diagnostic context.

## AI in the workflow, not just the algorithm

The most grounded AI conversations were less about replacing clinicians and more about **removing friction**: triage, structured reporting, prioritizing worklists, and quality control at capture. Human-in-the-loop was the recurring phrase.

## Our takeaway

The field is converging on richer capture and software-assisted reading. Multispectral imaging sits naturally at that intersection — capturing a denser spectral record at the source, and giving AI more to work with. We left more convinced than ever that **the source data, not just the model, is where the next gains come from.**
